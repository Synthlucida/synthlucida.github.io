New: Progrese — Free Chord Progression Generator with MIDI Export

SYNTHLUCIDA welcomes a new tool: Progrese, a free online chord progression generator. Pick a key and a mood — happy, sad, dreamy, epic, chill/lo-fi, dark, romantic or nostalgic — and it builds a musically coherent 8-bar chord progression complete with a matching melody and bassline, all built strictly from chords diatonic to the chosen key and scale.

You can play the result right in your browser, roll a new variation while keeping the same key and mood, or re-roll just the melody or bass while keeping the chords in place. When you're happy with it, export it as a standard MIDI file (3 tracks: chords, melody, bass) to use freely in your DAW — no sign-up, no attribution required.

Everything SYNTHLUCIDA Can Do: The Complete Feature Round-Up

SYNTHLUCIDA has quietly grown from a music player into a whole little ecosystem — one built around a single idea: helping you slow down, focus, play, or drift off, all for free, right in your browser. Here's everything currently packed into it, gathered in one place.

🎧 RELAX PLAYER — Three curated playlists (ASMR, PIANO, CHILL) wrapped in 20 animated visual themes, from NEON and AURORA to AMBER and MATRIX. Layer in WEATHER (a warm SUN scene, gentle RAIN, quiet WINTER snow or a windier WINTER II blizzard) and SUNSET/NIGHT skies on top of any theme. Open BREATHE for a full guided breathing session — 4-7-8, Box, Coherent, Calm or Sigh — and set CROSSFADE to 3-10 seconds for silky track transitions. 🕘 HISTORY tracks what you've played, 🎵 MY PLAYLIST lets you heart tracks, reorder them, and export or import your list, while an XP and leveling system quietly rewards daily listening with real MP3 downloads. Upload your own tracks in 📁 MY FILES and mix them into custom playlists, then SAVE your setup, go FULLSCREEN, download playlists for offline listening, or turn on gentle 🔔 REMINDERS to nudge you back for a break.

🧩 SYNTHLUCIDA BLOCKS (Game) — A line-clearing puzzle game where every line you clear adds to a permanent Career Lines total, which unlocks brand new ambient tracks across the ASMR, PIANO and CHILL categories. Every 20 Career Lines earns a 💣 BOMB charge and kicks off a 60-second Bonus Round at 2x score, while your Level - and the falling speed - climbs every 10 lines. 🌌 RELAX CANVAS (Night Sky) — An interactive ambient instrument set against a living night sky. Layer BINAURAL waves (Delta, Theta, Alpha), a NATURE/cricket loop, and a tap-to-play generative synth, then build full tracks in the 32-step SEQUENCER with independent KICK and MELODY layers. A deep sound design panel covers scales, oscillators, filter, decay, reverb, delay and drive, each with its own toggle. Set a SLEEP TIMER, hit REC to export a WAV recording, and watch the sky itself shift through real dawn, day, dusk and night in sync with your clock, complete with accurate moon phases, shooting stars, satellites and real constellations.

However you like to unwind — listening, playing, drawing or building your own soundscape — it's all connected, and it all lives in one place.

Relax Player: Audio Crackling Fix on Mobile

A few days ago a limiter (DynamicsCompressor) was added to stop digital clipping when several sound layers stack up past 0dBFS — that part was already working fine. But some phones, notably the Xiaomi Redmi Note 15 Pro+ 5G, kept crackling occasionally even after that fix, while the same session ran clean on a PC or a Motorola. It turned out to be a completely different issue: a buffer underrun, where the phone's audio thread can't recompute the next block of samples in time, leaving an audible gap or click. A limiter can't fix that, since it's a performance problem, not a loudness one — and HyperOS/MIUI is known for handling background audio scheduling less consistently than closer-to-stock Android, even on a chip that's stronger on paper.

Two real sources of unnecessary load were found in the code. The reverb (convolution) was running constantly even when switched off — every note and kick was still being sent through a 3.5-second convolution impulse regardless of the toggle, with the volume only zeroed out afterward. And the AudioContext wasn't given a latency hint, so the browser defaulted to the smallest, most sensitive buffer size, leaving almost no room for one slow frame before it became audible.

What changed: the AudioContext now requests a "playback" latency hint for a larger internal buffer and much more headroom against the odd slow frame (latency goes up slightly, imperceptible on ambient pads); reverb is only routed into the mix when it's actually turned on, so switching it off now truly costs nothing; and the reverb's impulse response has been shortened from 3.5s to 2.5s — still a long, lush tail, just a bit lighter to compute when reverb is on.
