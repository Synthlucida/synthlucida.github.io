How it works

01

Pick a key and mood

The key sets the starting note; the mood (happy, sad, dreamy, epic, chill/lo-fi, dark) sets the major or minor mode, tempo, and typical character of the progression.

02

The tool builds chords only from the scale

The progression is built exclusively from chords diatonic to the chosen key and scale — no random or foreign notes. That's why it always sounds coherent and "in tune," whatever combination comes up.

03

Melody and bass are derived from the chords

The melody draws on the tones of the current chord (triad/seventh chord) in rhythmic patterns, while the bassline plays the root, fifth, and octave — both always stay in harmony with the chords above. Pick a specific melody or bass style from the dropdowns, or leave them on "Auto" to get a different pattern on every bar.

04

Play it, or export it

The Play button starts a 6-bar loop right in your browser. MIDI export downloads a .mid file with three tracks (chords / melody / bass) that you can open in any DAW and build on.

Tip: "New variation" keeps the key and mood unchanged and just rolls a different progression from the same family — handy when you like the mood but want to try different chords. "New melody" and "New bass" re-roll just that part while keeping the current chords in place, so you can mix and match. The exported MIDI file can be freely transposed in your DAW, looped longer, or have its sounds swapped for your own.

Frequently asked questions

Is the chord generator free?

Yes, the tool is completely free and runs right in your browser, no sign-up needed.

Are the generated progressions musically correct?

Yes — every progression is built solely from chords diatonic to the chosen key and scale, so it always sounds coherent and "in tune." The melody and bassline are generated from the tones of those chords.

What format is the export?

The export is a standard MIDI file (.mid, format 1, 480 tpqn) with three tracks — chords, melody, and bass — compatible with all major DAWs.

Which moods can I choose from?

Happy, sad, dreamy, epic, chill/lo-fi, and dark — each mood has its own tempo, key mode, and set of typical progressions.
