About the app

VAPETRACK is a free web app for tracking e-cigarette puffs. No registration, no account, no server – everything is calculated and stored right in this browser.

Why this app was made

The idea for VAPETRACK came from personal experience with vaping. It's very easy to lose track of how much you actually vape in a day – puffs happen almost automatically, without thinking, and nicotine consumption quietly creeps up without you really noticing. That lack of awareness feeds the addiction itself, since without concrete numbers it's hard to realize just how much things have escalated.

So I decided to build a tool that tracks puffs and overall nicotine consumption in detail – so you can get a real picture of your habit and have a genuine chance to bring your nicotine addiction back under control. VAPETRACK is completely free and available to anyone who wants to keep their consumption in check and try to tame this addiction.

Logging
  • One tap on the main button adds 5 puffs
  • Today’s count and the time (plus “X min ago”) of your last puff are always visible
  • The “undo last entry” button removes the last addition if you tap by mistake
  • Individual entries can also be deleted later in the history
Charts and trends
  • A bar chart of the last 14 days with the average marked
  • A yearly heatmap of the last 365 days – scroll sideways to spot patterns over time
  • Hourly distribution over the last 7 days – when you puff most often
  • Comparison of this week with last week (up/down in %)
  • Your busiest hour of the day
Daily goal
  • An optional daily puff limit with a colored progress bar
  • The app only shows the numbers, it never judges or advises – it’s purely informational
XP and levels

VAPETRACK includes a simple game layer that rewards long-term reduction of consumption – not individual "good" days, but a real trend.

  • XP isn't stored as a separate number anywhere – it's always recalculated from your full entry history, so it's always accurate and works correctly even after restoring from a backup
  • The system needs at least 8 days of history before any XP shows up – only then does it have enough data to work out your "usual" daily baseline
  • For every elapsed day (today doesn't count yet, since it hasn't ended), your puff count is compared to the average of the previous 7 days
  • If a day is below that average, you earn XP proportional to how much lower it was – the bigger the drop, the more points
  • A full day with zero puffs earns a large bonus
  • A day at or above your average earns no XP, but also no penalty – a worse day never sets you back
  • XP accumulates across your entire history (up to 365 days back), so your level only keeps rising over time if your consumption actually trends down
  • The streak shows how many days in a row you've stayed below your average – it appears from 2 days in a row onward
  • Each level has its own title (Rookie, Observer, Determined… up to Legend), and the app notifies you whenever you level up
Controls
  • Switch between Czech and English (CZ/EN) near the top of the page
  • 5 color themes (the "Theme" button in the header) to match your taste
  • Export to PDF and CSV, plus backup and restore in JSON format – see the section below

The app doesn’t send any personal data to a server – everything stays in your browser until you download a backup yourself.

Vaping and reducing nicotine

A short overview of how nicotine addiction works and the approaches people most commonly use to cut back. This isn’t medical advice – just a summary of generally known principles.

How the addiction forms

After inhalation, nicotine reaches the bloodstream within seconds and triggers the release of dopamine in the brain, producing a brief feeling of satisfaction. The body gradually gets used to this dose and needs it more and more often – that's how physical dependence forms, layered on top of learned habits and rituals (a puff after a meal, with coffee, when stressed). So quitting isn't just about the nicotine itself, but also about those triggers.

Gradually lowering the strength
  • The most common approach is lowering the e-liquid's nicotine strength in small steps (e.g. every 1–2 weeks) instead of dropping to zero all at once
  • This lets the body adapt gradually, and withdrawal symptoms tend to be milder
  • Risk: at a lower strength, people tend to vape more often or take harder puffs to make up for the lower dose – so it's worth tracking total puff count too, not just the mg in the liquid
Reducing frequency and ritual
  • Stretching the interval between puffs by a few minutes at a time, gradually up to hours
  • Recognizing your typical triggers (after a meal, with coffee, in the car, when stressed) and consciously replacing them with something else – a walk, a glass of water, gum, a few deep breaths
  • Tracking your own data (like in this app) helps you see the real trend instead of the feeling of "I don't actually vape that much"
What to keep in mind
  • Quitting isn't linear – worse days are normal, and what matters is the long-term direction, not any single day
  • Alcohol and stress are the most common relapse triggers
  • Sudden complete cessation ("cold turkey") works well for some people, while others do better with a gradual reduction – there's no one-size-fits-all method
When to seek help

If you've struggled for a long time to cut back on your own, or you're experiencing strong withdrawal symptoms, it's worth reaching out to a doctor or a dedicated smoking-cessation service. This text is not medical advice and doesn't replace a professional consultation.
